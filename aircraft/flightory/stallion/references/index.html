⚠ Builder Notes — Lessons Learned & Gotchas

These observations were identified during construction, configuration, ground testing, and hover testing of Stallion VTOL V1 Build 01. They do not replace Flightory, Matek, or ArduPilot documentation; they record practical issues encountered on the actual aircraft.

Flight Controller Orientation

Verify the physical orientation of the Matek F765-Wing and the corresponding ArduPilot board-orientation configuration. Do not assume the installed direction matches ArduPilot defaults. Confirm the artificial horizon and reported attitude respond correctly when the aircraft is manually rolled, pitched, and yawed.

Lesson: A mechanically correct FC installation can still result in incorrect stabilization when board orientation is configured incorrectly.

Control Surface Direction — Two Separate Checks

Before flight, verify every aerodynamic control surface using both pilot stick commands and ArduPilot stabilization corrections. These are separate checks. A surface can respond correctly to the transmitter yet still move incorrectly when the autopilot attempts to stabilize the aircraft.

Lesson: Correct transmitter response alone does not establish correct stabilized-flight behavior.

VTOL Pitch Direction

During early hover testing, VTOL pitch response was discovered to be reversed. Explicitly verify that VTOL pitch correction opposes aircraft movement before sustained hover testing.

Lesson: Verify VTOL stabilization direction independently from transmitter-response testing.

Motor Assignment

Positively identify each VTOL motor and verify that the corresponding ArduPilot output operates the intended physical motor.

Servo OutputArduPilot FunctionPhysical Motor
S7Motor1Front Right Tilt Motor
S8Motor2Front Left Tilt Motor
S9Motor4Rear Fixed Lift Motor
Lesson: Never determine motor identity from wiring position or assumption. Test outputs individually.

Motor Rotation, Propeller Orientation & Thrust Direction

Correct output mapping does not guarantee correct thrust. Individually verify motor rotation, propeller type/orientation, installation direction, and actual thrust direction. Repeat these checks whenever a motor, ESC, propeller, or motor connection is changed.

Tilt Servo Direction & Synchronization

Both front tilt mechanisms must move in the correct direction, remain synchronized, reach the intended VTOL/forward-flight positions, and operate without mechanical binding.

Tilt Endpoints

Tilt-servo MIN/MAX values required adjustment during development. Set endpoints according to the actual mechanical installation and check both extremes for binding, servo buzzing/load, linkage interference, unequal motor position, and excessive travel.

Lesson: Endpoint configuration is both a flight-control setting and a mechanical-protection setting.

ESC Signal Wiring

Each Holybro Tekko32 F4 45A ESC uses Signal + Ground only at the FC control connection. ESC red/BEC wires are not connected.

There is no separate PDB. The 4S flight battery connects to the Matek F765-Wing battery input. The three ESC power leads are supplied from the split high-current power lead associated with the flight-controller power connection.

  • S7 → Motor1 → Front Right
  • S8 → Motor2 → Front Left
  • S9 → Motor4 → Rear Lift
Lesson: Document high-current power wiring separately from ESC signal/ground wiring.

FPV Power & Installed Equipment

The current V1 analog FPV installation uses the Caddx Turtle V2 camera and ImmersionRC 2.4 GHz VTX. Both receive power from the Matek F765-Wing. No buzzer is installed.

Lesson: Document the aircraft as actually installed, not equipment previously tested or considered.

ArduPilot SERIAL Numbers vs Physical UARTs

ArduPilot SERIALx logical numbering does not numerically correspond to the STM32 physical UART/USART number on the Matek F765-Wing. When troubleshooting a peripheral, record all three layers:

ArduPilot SERIAL port → Physical UART/USART → Matek board pads

Lesson: Do not assume, for example, that SERIAL1 means physical UART1.

Roll & Pitch PID Oscillation

Early hover testing exhibited oscillation. Q_A_RAT_RLL and Q_A_RAT_PIT tuning was required before the aircraft produced an acceptable hover.

Lesson: Successfully leaving the ground does not establish that the controller is adequately tuned.

Known-Good PID Values Are Starting Points

Another Stallion builder's PID configuration was useful as an initial baseline, but still required validation and tuning on this aircraft. AUW, CG, motors, propellers, servos, linkages, battery, structural stiffness, and component placement can change the required tuning.

Lesson: Another aircraft's successful settings are an engineering reference, not a substitute for validation.

Yaw Authority

Yaw authority required progressive tuning. Q_A_RAT_YAW_P was increased incrementally while flight behavior and BIN data were evaluated, with approximately 0.22 becoming the current V1 baseline during this phase of development.

Lesson: Make tuning changes incrementally and retain previous known-good configurations.

QSTABILIZE Does Not Establish QLOITER Readiness

A controllable QSTABILIZE hover does not automatically mean the aircraft is ready for QLOITER. Before progressing, confirm attitude control, yaw authority, GPS health, compass behavior, EKF health, position-estimate stability, motor saturation, vibration, and sensor behavior.

Lesson: Treat QLOITER as the next validation stage, not simply another switch position.

Magnetic Interference

Mission Planner has repeatedly reported “Magnetic field varies with throttle.” Do not treat this only as a binary warning. Engineering analysis should consider magnetic-field range, throttle correlation, yaw innovation, yaw/throttle correlation, EKF behavior, current, and throttle.

The #teamcorc Engineering Toolkit is being enhanced to consolidate these measurements into a Magnetic Interference Index / Compass Health score.

Lesson: Determine operational significance from quantitative flight data rather than warning text alone.

Record the Configuration for Every Flight Test

Each flight record should identify the actual test configuration, including AUW, battery, firmware, PARAM/configuration baseline, changes since the previous flight, flight modes, test objective, observations, BIN log, and video when available.

Change One Thing at a Time

Whenever practical, make one tuning or configuration change at a time and conduct a controlled test before proceeding.

Lesson: Controlled iteration preserves cause/effect traceability.

Preserve PARAM Baselines

Save named PARAM snapshots after meaningful milestones such as initial configuration, motor/servo verification, first hover, roll/pitch changes, yaw changes, first stable hover, QLOITER preparation, first successful QLOITER, pre-transition configuration, and post-transition tuning.

Preserve BIN Logs

Retain BIN logs for meaningful tests. They enable quantitative evaluation of PID tracking, EKF behavior, yaw performance, motor saturation, magnetic interference, RC input, GPS, battery behavior, mode changes, and system messages.

Lesson: Video shows what the aircraft appeared to do; the BIN log helps establish why it did it.

Treat the Build Record as Configuration Control

The documentation should describe the aircraft as it actually existed for a particular test. Update the engineering record whenever hardware, firmware, wiring, parameters, battery configuration, or other significant items change.

Lesson: Accurate configuration control is essential when comparing later flights against earlier BIN logs, videos, and tuning results.
